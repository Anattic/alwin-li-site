Professional

A working life, in order.

Not a resume. The sequence that actually taught me something.

01

Francis Lewis High School

Queens

I didn’t arrive at finance through a brochure. I arrived through a high school that treated rank, contests, and organizations as real things — not extras.

JROTC made accountability feel physical before it was theoretical. I finished as a Second Lieutenant. Alongside that: Virtual Enterprises National Business Plan Finalist, National Economics Challenge, National Personal Finance Challenge, Esports State Championship Finalist, and AP Scholar with Distinction. 02

NüApps

CFO  ·  Sept 2022 – Sept 2025

I founded and led NüApps as CFO — an AI healthcare venture that put me on the other side of a capital conversation for three years. I built the financial models and forecasting tools, and I lived with what those models missed. We cut operational inefficiencies by about 30% and grew revenue 40%.

The useful lesson wasn’t the percentages. It was how quickly capital, once raised, becomes a set of tradeoffs you have to stand behind.

03

Remedi

Health-tech  ·  EY Ground Floor Challenge

Remedi was the next company I built: a health-tech startup that reached the finals of the EY Ground Floor Challenge. Same muscle, different shape — how you talk about a business when someone else is deciding whether it’s worth their time.

04

Gabelli School of Business  ·  Expected May 2029

Personal

How I actually think.

Finance interested me for the same reason philosophy did: both are about what people do when the rules are incomplete. I like the math. I stay for the decision — who is incentivized to do what, what that does to the outcome, and the moment a model stops being enough and you have to choose anyway.

Building NüApps and Remedi put me on the founder’s side of the table. Capital isn’t a product there. It’s runway, and it’s what you decline. Three years of models and forecasts, of pulling inefficiency out of operations, of standing in a room at the EY Ground Floor Challenge and asking someone to believe the business — that sits with me now when I look at a deal from the banking side. I know what it feels like to ask. I know how a clean spreadsheet can still be a preference in disguise.

The competitive streak is older than any of that. Virtual Enterprises, the National Economics Challenge, the Personal Finance Challenge: I wasn’t collecting medals. I wanted to understand how money and organizations actually work, and contests were the first place that question had a scoreboard. High school JROTC did a similar thing in a different register — rank, follow-through, the difference between saying you lead and having to.

In September 2023 I co-founded Outfit Outreach. We organize clothing drives — more than fifteen of them — and have diverted over two thousand pounds of clothing to shelters, churches, and families who needed it. The organization has grown past 150 members. Directing surplus toward a community is a different allocation problem than a bond deal. It still teaches you something about what people do with resources, and why.

Across those rooms I’ve seen capital from several angles: raising it, deploying it into a small business, directing it toward a neighborhood, and structuring billions of it as a banker. The through-line is judgment. That’s the work I want more of.
